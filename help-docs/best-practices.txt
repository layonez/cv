**TL;DR**: Use a clean 1-page, single-column resume (2 pages only if 10+ years). Write impact-first bullets with STAR/CAR/XYZ, quantify results, and tailor keywords to the JD. Show open-source proof on GitHub/GitLab and link it. Keep formatting ATS-safe: standard headings, black text, no icons, no tables/columns, right-aligned dates. EU nuance: longer CVs and headshots are more accepted. ([Reddit][1])

### Step-by-step best practices pulled from subreddit wikis and hiring blogs

1. **Format for readability and ATS**

* Single column. No icons, graphics, tables, or multi-column layouts. Font ≥10.5, black text, generous white space and margins. Don’t justify text. ([Reddit][1])
* Keep to **1 page per decade** of experience; 2 pages only if you’re senior/staff with dense relevant impact. ([Reddit][1])
* Use standard section headers. Avoid fancy labels. Prefer text-based PDF or DOCX; ensure the PDF is selectable text. Avoid headers/footers that hide content from parsers. ([indeed.com][2])
* Dates: use “Present”, spell months or standard abbrev, use an **en dash** with spaces, right-align dates; bullets should not extend past the date column. ([Reddit][1])
* **EU nuance**: European CVs are often longer; Europass template is accepted but plain. Headshots are common in some countries. ([Reddit][3])
* **Myth check**: Modern ATS aren’t pure auto-reject robots; humans still review. Tailor, don’t try to “game” ATS. ([Lever][4])

2. **Write high-signal bullets**

* Use STAR/CAR/XYZ. One sentence per bullet, 1–2 lines, no personal pronouns, no ending periods. Order bullets by relevance and impressiveness. ([Reddit][1])
* Examples:

  * “Cut CI time **35%** by caching pnpm deps and parallelizing Cypress runs.”
  * “Drove **+28%** SDK adoption by overhauling docs, adding typed examples, and a migration guide.”
* r/resumes also endorses PAR/STAR and result-first patterns. ([Reddit][5])

3. **Prioritize sections correctly**

* Put your most compelling, relevant content at the top-left of the page. Tailor to each JD with its language and context. ([Reddit][1])
* **Work experience**: achievements and context, not job duties; add tangible metrics. Clearly mark internships or contracts. ([Reddit][1])
* **Skills**: keep readable lists, avoid multi-column grids, no “skill meters”. Focus on capabilities behind tools, not just tool names. ([Reddit][1])
* **Projects**: bullets, not paragraphs; order by relevance; link to repos. ([Reddit][1])
* **Senior/Staff**: brief 1–2 sentence summary is OK; keep ≤2 pages; maintain separate IC vs. management versions; note influence as well as impact. ([Reddit][1])

4. **Make it ATS-safe without overthinking**

* Use simple layout and standard headings; avoid graphics and columns; consistency in dates and formatting matters. ([Jobscan][6])
* Keywords still matter because recruiters **search** by them; mirror the JD where accurate. ([Jobscan][7])

5. **Dev-tools companies (GitLab, GitHub) care about OSS proof**

* Link GitHub/GitLab. Pin repos. Show merged PRs, issues triaged, docs you shipped, packages released, stars/downloads, and community impact. GitLab explicitly states strong OSS presence helps candidates. ([about.gitlab.com][8])
* GitHub’s own career content advises **quantifying impact** and optimizing your GitHub profile; they run resume-polish sessions at Universe. ([The GitHub Blog][9])

7. **What to leave out**

* Random hobbies unrelated to the role, over-bolding, ALL CAPS, italics spam, visually clever but unreadable layouts. ([Reddit][1])

---

### Useful considerations

* **Link hygiene**: make all links clickable and human-readable (GitHub profile, portfolio, key repos, npm packages, talks). Many ATS preserve URLs. ([indeed.com][2])
* **Context lines**: for each company, 1 short line with product, scale, and stack helps reviewers parse impact fast. ([Reddit][1])
* **EU specifics per country**: check if headshots or longer CVs are expected where you apply; adjust versions accordingly. ([Reddit][3])
